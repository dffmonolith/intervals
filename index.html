Intervals is an ear-training tool for singers. It plays a note on a real grand piano and either asks you to sing an interval from it — the microphone listens and tells you how close you got — or plays an interval and asks you to name it. Both can be done by interval name or by solfège syllable in a key. A built-in Tuner shows you exactly where your pitch sits, note by note.

Getting started

  1. Pick a mode at the top right: Sing, Name it or Tuner. For Sing and Name it, choose Intervals or Solfège, and which way the intervals go: ↑ Up, ↓ Down or ↕ Both. You can change these any time; a fresh question starts right away.
  2. Open Settings (the button at the top, beside ?) and choose your voice part and the intervals you want to work on. (The defaults are a good place to begin.)
  3. Press Start. In Sing and Tuner, your browser will ask to use the microphone — allow it. The first time, Intervals offers a quick microphone check (see Settings) so it can hear you properly.
Headphones help a lot in Sing mode. Through speakers, the microphone hears the piano as well as you. Intervals waits for the piano to die away before it listens, but headphones make everything cleaner.

Name it doesn’t use the microphone at all, so it works anywhere — on a train, in a library, or when the mic won’t cooperate.

Sing mode

You hear a reference note, and the prompt tells you what to sing — for example “Sing a major 3rd above.” Sing the note and hold it steady. The thin bar under the pitch trace fills as you hold; when it’s full, you’re graded.

  • Intervals ignores the first moment of each note (the little scoop most of us make coming in), then takes the middle of what you held as your note.
  • A right answer flashes a green ✓; a miss shows an amber ✗ and tells you what you sang — how many cents sharp or flat, or, if you landed on a different note, which interval you actually sang.
▶ Reference
Plays the starting note again.
Hear answer
Plays the reference and then the target. If you use it before you’ve sung, that question won’t count as right — it’s shown as a hollow dot in your progress.
Try again
Sing the same question once more. Only your first try counts toward accuracy, but getting it on a retry is noted.
Next →
A new question. After a right answer, Intervals can go on by itself (see Next question in Settings); a violet bar counts down the pause.
■ Stop
Silences the piano, ends the drill and turns the microphone off. If you sang anything, a How close you were report opens (see below).

The staff

Beside the prompt, a staff in your voice part’s clef shows the starting note as it sounds. Once you’re graded, your note lands where you actually sang it — green if right, amber if not, with the target beside it in cyan so you can see the gap. In Intervals style there’s no key signature, and the two notes are spelled so the interval reads as written (a 3rd three letters up, a 5th five). In Name it, the second note appears once you’ve answered.

How close you were

When you press Stop (or Esc) after a Sing session, a report (which you can turn off in Settings › Display) shows every question you sang: what was asked, and a bar from 100 cents flat to 100 cents sharp with the green “counts as right” band. Your first try is a solid dot, retries are hollow rings. If you landed on a different note altogether, the dot is pinned to the end with a ring and the note you sang is named on the right. At the top: how many questions, how many were right first time, your average distance from the target, and whether you tend to sit sharp or flat.

Solfège

Above the prompt, switch Intervals | Solfège. Solfège works in both Sing and Name it, and drills the way a choir sight-reads: in a major key, by syllable, with movable do — whatever key you choose, its first note is do, and the scale runs do re mi fa sol la ti do.

Choose the Key from the menu beside the switch; the scale is spelled out underneath, so you can see where do is. Every question stays in that key until you change it.

What you hear

A row of labelled chips lights up as each sound plays, so you always know what you’re hearing:

  1. I–IV–V⁷–I — a cadence to set the key. It plays at the start and whenever you change key (or before every question, if you choose that in Settings). ▶ Key (or K) plays it again whenever you’ve lost the key.
  2. do — the do at or just below the starting note.
  3. The starting note, labelled with its syllable and marked start — for example mi · B3 · start. (If the start is do itself, you hear it once.)

Sing · Solfège

Once do and the starting note have sounded, the prompt shows what to sing: “Sing sol (perfect 4th above re)”. Sing it and hold it, as in Sing mode. The big readout shows the syllable you’re singing with the note name under it, and the pitch trace is labelled in syllables. ▶ do + start plays do and the starting note again; Hear answer plays the start and the target.

Under the prompt is a staff (see below for the clef): do and the starting note appear on it as they sound, and once your note is graded it lands where you actually sang it — green if you hit the target, amber if not, with the target beside it in cyan so you can see how far off you were. Hear answer puts the target on the staff as it plays; Try again clears your note.

Name it · Solfège

After do and the starting note comes a mystery note (a ? chip). Tap its syllable. The answer buttons are the scale in order, each with its note name for this key. After answering, tap any syllable to hear it from the starting note and compare.

Beside the buttons is a staff (the same one Sing · Solfège shows) in the key you chose, with the clef for your voice (treble; treble with a little 8 below for tenor, which sounds an octave lower than written; bass clef for baritone and bass). do and the starting note appear on it as they sound, and the mystery note joins them once you answer — green if you got it, amber if not. Watching where the notes sit on the staff while you hear them is a good way to get to know a key: what fa looks like in E♭, how far sol is above do. (If Settings says both notes together, the start and the mystery note sound at once.)

The reference is always one of the seven scale notes. The target normally is too; tick include the chromatic syllables in Settings and targets can also be di (raised do), me, fi, le or te (lowered mi, raised fa, lowered la, lowered ti). Your choices of intervals, direction and voice apply here too.

Working on a piece in a minor key? Sing it with la-based minor: choose the relative major (for A minor, C major), and the minor tonic is la.

Name it mode

You hear two notes — one after the other, or both together, depending on Settings — and tap the button for the interval you heard. Each button shows the short name (m3, P5…) and the full name underneath.

  • The right answer lights up green. If you missed, your choice turns amber and the question is played again so you can listen.
  • After you’ve answered, tap any interval button to hear that interval from the same starting note. It’s the quickest way to learn the difference between two intervals you keep confusing.
  • ▶ Play again replays the question as many times as you like.

Tuner

Sing any note and the Tuner shows which note you’re on and how many cents sharp or flat you are. To aim for a particular pitch, tap one of the note buttons; ▶ Play sounds it on the piano (the Tuner doesn’t listen while the piano is playing).

The buttons cover one voice part’s range, octave included (for Alto, G3 up to C5). The Vocal range button shows the range on a staff, with the note you’ve picked in cyan. Tap it to choose Soprano, Alto, Tenor, Baritone or Bass. It starts as your voice in Settings each time you open the Tuner, so you can borrow another part’s range for a while (to stretch your top, say) without changing your voice for the drills.

Each note you hold is an attempt. When you stop singing, or move to another note, you get a summary: the note, how long you held it, how sharp or flat you were on average, how much the pitch wavered, and what share of the time you were in tune. Your last eight attempts stay below as small chips — green ones were in tune.

Good exercise: pick a note, hold it for five seconds, and watch the “wavered” number come down as your support steadies.

Settings

Tap Settings at the top. Changes take effect straight away; Done, ×, Esc or a tap outside closes the window.

Voice
Keeps both the reference and the answer inside a comfortable range for Soprano, Alto, Tenor, Baritone or Bass (each button shows its range on a staff in that part’s clef: treble for soprano and alto, treble with a little 8 below for tenor, bass clef for baritone and bass). It also sets the clef on the staff, and the Tuner’s starting range.
Intervals
Tick the ones to practise (the direction — up, down or both — is chosen on the main panel). All, Consonant and Steps are quick presets. Starting with a few and adding more as they become easy works well.
Singing counts as right
How close is close enough: ±15 cents is strict, ±25 is the default, ±40 is lenient.
Octave
Accept the right note sung in a different octave — useful for low voices answering a high reference, or high voices a low one.
Hold for
How long you must hold the note before it’s graded.
Name it plays
The two notes one after the other (melodic) or together (harmonic). Harmonic is harder — try it once melodic is solid.
Next question
Go on by itself after a right answer, after a 1-, 2-, 3- or 5-second pause.
Display
Turn off the How close you were report at the end of a Sing session, and/or hide Your progress. Results are still recorded while it’s hidden — switch it back on to see them, or to use Reset.
Solfège
Add the chromatic syllables (di, me, fi, le, te), and/or play the I–IV–V⁷–I cadence before every question instead of only at the start and when the key changes.
Microphone
Check my microphone: sing “ah” at your normal practice volume for a couple of seconds and Intervals sets the Boost to suit your voice and your device. Do it again if you change device, headphones or room — or if it keeps saying too quiet. (The Check button beside Boost does the same.)
Troubleshooting
Shows technical microphone details under the Mic bar; handy if something isn’t working.

Reading the display

Big note
The note you’re singing right now, with its frequency in hertz.
avg
Under it, the running average of how far off you are on the note you’re holding — how many cents sharp or flat — so you can see whether you tend to sit high or low. It stays (dimmed) after you stop. The session line averages all your sung answers since you opened the page.
Meter
How far you are from the target, from −100 to +100 cents. The green band is the “counts as right” zone.
Trace
The last six seconds of your pitch. In Sing mode the dashed violet line is the reference and the green line and band are the target; your line turns green while you’re in the zone.
Mic bar
How loud the microphone hears you. Next to it: hearing you (a clear pitch), listening (sound, but no clear pitch yet), too quiet, silent or off.
Boost
Turns up the microphone so you can sing softly. If it keeps saying too quiet, raise it (it can’t distort), or tap Check and let Intervals choose.
What’s a cent? A hundredth of a semitone. Most listeners start to notice a note is out of tune somewhere around 10–20 cents; a choir that holds within ±25 sounds in tune.

Your progress

Every question is recorded. Choose This session, Today or All time, and Sung, Named, Solfège (sung or named by syllable) or All.

  • The tiles show trials, right answers, accuracy, your current and best streak, and how many cents off you were on average when singing.
  • The row of dots is your recent history, oldest first: right, missed, heard the answer first. Hover (or long-press) a dot for details.
  • The table breaks it down by interval and direction, and Mixed up with shows what you tend to sing or choose instead — that’s where to practise next.
  • Export CSV downloads every result as a spreadsheet. Reset erases them.

Don’t want to see it? Settings › Display › show my progress hides this panel; results are still kept.

Results stay in this browser on this device — they aren’t sent anywhere, and a different browser or device starts fresh. On iPhone and iPad, Intervals on the home screen counts as a different browser: it keeps its own results, separate from Safari’s. Use Export CSV if you want to keep a copy of the old ones.

Install on your phone

Intervals can live on your home screen like an app: its own icon, the whole screen to itself, and it works with no internet connection. Nothing to download from an app store.

  • iPhone or iPad: open Intervals in Safari and tap Share (the square with an arrow) › Add to Home Screen › Add.
  • Android: in Chrome, tap the ⋮ menu › Add to Home screen (or Install app).
  • Computer: in Chrome or Edge, click the install icon at the right of the address bar.

Open it once while you’re online. When ✓ offline ready appears beside the version number at the top, everything it needs is saved on the device and it will run anywhere — a rehearsal room with no signal, a train, a plane. Whenever it opens with a connection it checks for a newer version and uses that.

An iPhone may ask for the microphone again each time you open the home-screen app; that’s Apple’s rule for web apps. Your results there are kept separately from Safari’s (see Your progress).

Troubleshooting

The microphone won’t start

Browsers only allow the microphone on a secure page (an address beginning https://). If you refused permission by mistake: on iPad or iPhone, tap aA in Safari’s address bar › Website Settings › Microphone › Allow, then press Start again; in Chrome or Edge, click the icon at the left of the address bar. Name it always works without a mic.

It says “too quiet”, or ignores me

Run the microphone check (Settings, or Check beside Boost) — it sets Boost for your voice. Or raise Boost yourself, move a little closer, and sing on a clear vowel (“ah” or “oo”). Consonants, breaths and whispering don’t have a pitch to measure.

It says the microphone is sending only silence

Tap Restart mic in the message. If that doesn’t help, reload the page. Closing other apps that use the microphone (video calls, voice memos) can also help.

No sound at all on iPhone or iPad

Check the volume buttons first. Intervals is designed to play even in Silent Mode; if yours still stays quiet, turn Silent Mode off (the switch on the side, or the bell in Control Center), reload the page and try again — and let us know which device and iOS version.

The sound stopped on my iPad

iPadOS pauses web audio after a phone call, a notification or switching apps. Just tap anywhere on the page to wake it. Also check that the ringer/silent switch or Control Center isn’t muting sound.

Android phones and tablets

Use Chrome (or Samsung Internet) and allow the microphone when asked. If you refused it: tap the icon just left of the web address › Permissions › Microphone › Allow, and reload. Bluetooth headphones are fine for listening, but if the phone uses the headset’s own microphone it drops into call-quality sound — wired headphones work best. If the mic says it’s busy, close any app that might be using it (a call, a voice recorder, the voice assistant).

It stopped when I locked the screen or switched apps

That’s deliberate. When the page goes into the background, Intervals stops the drill, turns off the microphone and lets go of the sound, so it doesn’t keep running (and draining the battery) behind your back. Tap Start when you come back. While a drill is running, Intervals keeps the screen from dimming, so it won’t lock in the middle of a note.

It thinks I sang the wrong octave

Low voices’ overtones can sometimes fool a pitch detector. Choosing the right Voice in Settings helps; or tick Octave to accept the right note in any octave.

Something else

Tick Troubleshooting in Settings and note what the details line says — it shows what the microphone is really delivering.
